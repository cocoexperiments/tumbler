Two ways to use Tumbler

One keypad. Two very different jobs.

Choose a mode for each app. Same shuffling keypad, same PIN, different purpose.

Privacy

A lock for your private apps.

Hand someone your phone without handing them your chats. WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, photos, banking: each opens only with your PIN or fingerprint.

  • Shoulder-surf resistant. Tap positions say nothing about digits.
  • Re-locks when you leave. Screen off, or a grace window you pick.
  • Fingerprint if you like. The PIN keypad is always there.
Digital wellbeing

Friction that breaks the habit.

Most doom scrolling starts with a thumb that opens the app before you decide to. Tumbler adds a pause and a keypad you have to think about, so opening it becomes a choice.

  • A mindful pause. A short countdown before the keypad even works.
  • Time-boxed access. Open for 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes, then it locks again.
  • Can't be tapped on autopilot. The board reshuffles, so muscle memory doesn't help.
How it works

Tap the button that holds your digit.

Your PIN is four digits. You never type digits. You pick which group contains the next one.

  1. Create one 4-digit PIN

    One PIN covers every locked app. Set it once, confirm it, done.

  2. Choose your apps

    Switch on the ones to lock, then pick Privacy or Wellbeing for each.

  3. Open a locked app

    Tumbler steps in front of it with a freshly shuffled board.

  4. Tap the group with your digit

    Each digit sits on at least two buttons, so there is always more than one right answer. A wrong tap reshuffles.

  5. You're in

    Privacy apps open right away. Wellbeing apps open for the time you chose, then lock again.

Why Tumbler

Simple on the surface. Stubborn underneath.

Built around one idea, that your PIN shouldn't be readable from the outside, plus the details that make an app lock actually hold.

Shuffling digit groups

A 3×3 board where each button carries three digits. It reshuffles when the lock opens and after every wrong tap.

You pick the apps

Lock only what needs it. Everything else opens as normal, so Tumbler stays out of your way.

A mode for each app

Privacy asks for the PIN every time. Wellbeing adds a pause and a timed window. Set them app by app.

Brute-force lockout

After five wrong taps, escalating lockouts kick in: 30 seconds, then longer, up to 15 minutes.

Encrypted on-device

Your PIN lives in encrypted storage on your phone. No accounts, no servers, no analytics, no network calls.

Can't read your screen

Tumbler only notices which app is in front. It never reads what's inside any app.

Fingerprint or PIN, your choice

Turn on fingerprint unlock and touch the sensor to get into a locked app, or use the shuffling keypad. The PIN is always there as a fallback, and the first unlock after a restart always asks for it. Off by default.

Security, plainly

Built with honest trade-offs.

Tumbler is explicit about how it works and where its limits are.

Good to know: Android won't let an app read or reuse your phone's own PIN, so Tumbler has its own. That's what lets the keypad shuffle. A determined person with your unlocked phone could still uninstall Tumbler or switch off its Accessibility permission. That is fine as a speed bump for wellbeing, and worth knowing for privacy.
  • Needs one Accessibility permission. It's how Tumbler notices an app opening. It only sees which window is in front and can't read anything on your screen.
  • No network access to your PIN. Stored locally in encrypted preferences.
  • Fingerprint is optional. It uses Android's own biometric prompt, so Tumbler never sees your fingerprint. The PIN always works, and is required after a restart.
  • Your phone's lock is untouched. Tumbler doesn't replace or interfere with it.
  • Open source. Read every line on GitHub.

How to install
  1. Download the APK on your phone.
  2. Open it and allow “Install unknown apps” for your browser or file manager if asked.
  3. Open Tumbler and create your PIN.
  4. Grant “Display over other apps” and turn on the Tumbler Accessibility service.
  5. Choose the apps to lock and a mode for each, then open one to test it.
  6. Optional: turn on “Unlock with fingerprint” (needs a fingerprint enrolled in Android settings).
Accessibility switch greyed out?

On Android 13+, a sideloaded app needs one extra step: App info → ⋮ menu → Allow restricted settings. Then enable the Tumbler service.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Can I use my phone's PIN instead of a Tumbler PIN?

No. Android doesn't let apps read or check your system PIN, and its own prompt can't shuffle. Tumbler has one PIN of its own, shared by every locked app.

Do I need a different PIN for each app?

No. One PIN covers everything. What you choose per app is the mode (Privacy or Wellbeing) and the timing.

How is Wellbeing mode different from Privacy mode?

Privacy asks for your PIN or fingerprint each time. Wellbeing adds a short countdown before the keypad works, then lets you in for a window you choose (5 to 30 minutes) before locking again. It's built to make you pause, not to keep anyone out.

Why does it need Accessibility?

It's the only reliable way for an app to know another app just came to the front. Tumbler only watches which window is showing. It can't read your screen, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Can someone bypass it?

Someone who already has your unlocked phone could uninstall Tumbler or turn off its Accessibility permission. It stops casual snooping and impulse opens, not a determined attacker with full access to an unlocked phone.

Can I unlock with my fingerprint?

Yes, if you turn it on. You can then touch the sensor or use the PIN keypad. After a restart, the PIN is needed first.

Can someone guess my PIN by tapping?

Each tap narrows a digit to three possibilities and the board reshuffles after any mistake. After five wrong taps, Tumbler adds escalating lockouts of up to 15 minutes.

Is anything sent off my device?

No. There are no accounts or analytics, and your PIN is stored in encrypted storage on the phone.

What if I forget my PIN?

Reboot into Safe mode, where third-party apps are disabled, then clear or uninstall Tumbler.

Is it on Google Play?

Not yet. Apps that use Accessibility face strict Play review, so for now Tumbler is distributed as an APK on GitHub.
